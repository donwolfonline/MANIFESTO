Most of what we build is engineered to be forgotten by Tuesday. Feeds refresh, interfaces twitch for attention, and tools ask to be updated before they have ever been understood. This document is a small refusal—a plain-text anchor written in `Markdown`, meant to stay legible twenty years from now in any terminal, editor, or browser.

> Good work does not shout to prove it exists. It lowers the pulse of the room the moment you open it.

---

## 01 — Plain Text Outlives Everything

Every elaborate format eventually becomes a locked room whose key belonged to a company that no longer exists. Plain text has no vendor, no subscription tier, and no expiration date.

When an idea begins as clean prose in a `.md` file, it is forced to stand on its own architecture before color, chrome, or animation ever arrive. Ornament should illuminate structure—never disguise the lack of it.

### Rules of Legibility

1. **Write for a future reader with less patience than you.** Cut the throat-clearing; lead with what is true.
2. **Preserve the source.** The rendered page is a lens, not a cage. Anyone should be able to inspect the raw text underneath.
3. **Prefer files over feeds.** A directory of named artifacts compounds; an algorithmic stream evaporates.

---

## 02 — Whitespace Is Not Empty

Fear fills every margin. When we are uncertain that a sentence is strong enough, we add a badge beside it, a tooltip over it, and a banner above it.

Generous space is an act of trust. Setting body text to a calm `62ch` measure and surrounding it with silence tells the reader: *nothing here is trying to trap you*. Your eyes have room to rest between thoughts.

> Restraint is not minimalism for a gallery wall. It is hospitality for a tired mind.

---

## 03 — Speed Feels Like Respect

Latency is not merely a technical metric; it is a tone of voice. Software that hesitates feels indifferent. Software that responds at the edge of perception disappears into the hand, the way a well-balanced pen or a sharp kitchen knife disappears into the task.

- **Honor the hardware.** Never spend a user's battery to render an effect they did not ask for.
- **Keep motion purposeful.** Movement should clarify spatial continuity—where something came from and where it went—and step aside immediately when `prefers-reduced-motion` is set.
- **Let typography carry the weight.** Scale, rhythm, and contrast do more emotional work than ten thousand particles ever could.

---

## 04 — One Accent Is Enough

Color loses its voice the moment everything insists on speaking at once. Keep the ground warm, dark, and steady. Keep the ink soft enough to read for an hour without glare. Reserve a single accent solely for the few moments that genuinely earn a spark of heat.

```text
ground  -> #201e1f   (warm near-black, never sterile #000)
ink     -> #f2eee8   (unbleached paper off-white)
accent  -> #eb5e28   (used like a red pencil mark in the margin)
```

If everything is highlighted, nothing is important.

---

## 05 — Build Slow Things on Purpose

Urgency is easy to manufacture; permanence takes nerve. Choose problems worth sitting with for years. Leave small, thoughtful details in places only the attentive will notice—the alignment of a colophon, the curve of a transition, the cadence of a paragraph break.

> Make fewer things. Make them quieter. Make them last longer than the season that praised them.

This file is never finished. It is revised whenever my habits drift from my convictions, committed in plain text, and read again when the noise gets loud.
